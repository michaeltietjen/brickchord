# Changelog

## Unreleased: Brickglide
- **Brickglide**, a third app on the same engine: an ambient machine. Six drone voices on the D-pad, X and Y that fade in and out and can glide between notes over up to a minute; two melodic voices on A and B that step through five notes each (struck sounds like Kalimba restrike, sustained ones glide). The screen draws every voice's pitch over the last 24 seconds against guide lines for the scale, with a dashed guide showing where a gliding voice is headed. Each voice has its own sound (drones, bowed and blown resonators, bells, pads), note and cents, level, pan, glide, fade, vibrato, tremolo and random drift. Six scenes (SELECT + a drone button saves, R1 + it recalls, morphing over 1 to 60 s), R2 evolves every voice to a nearby note of the scale, START freezes the cloud, the left stick spreads the voices apart and deepens their movement, L2 + left stick plays Cloud. Installs as roms/ports/brickglide with Brickglide.sh.

## Unreleased
- Fewer clicks on the Brick, with no change to the sound: the launchers run the CPU at full speed while the app is open (and put the governor back after), the audio process keeps the garbage collector out of its way, Settings no longer re-reads demo and song files every frame, voice mixing costs about 45% less, long samples are decoded once and stored once per note, and a silent track with Cloud stops processing once its cloud is empty.

## v0.3.0 (Nov 3, 2026): Bricklab, machines, Macro, Cloud, demo songs
- Drone mode: hold L2 and the left stick plays Cloud on the drone. The direction picks one of the eight textures like a compass (up Shimmer, up-right Bloom, right Drift, down-right Haze, down Grit, down-left Dust, left Smear, up-left Freeze) and the distance from centre sets the amount. Let go of L2 and the cloud stays; tap L2 without moving the stick to turn it off. Without L2 the stick still pulses, swells, thins and blooms the drone. Both editions.
- Fix: changing Cloud's texture while grains were sounding crashed the audio (a grain kept its old length but got the new window).
- Cloud: Grit sounded like Dust, so it now earns its name: short dense grains, half of them reversed, wide in the stereo field, and the cloud bit-crushed and sample-held. Haze, Drift and Freeze also mix in some reversed grains.
- Settings, Bricklab: four tabs, SONGS, GLOBAL, the selected track's tab and FX. The track tab is named after the track and its machine (T3 LEAD, T1 DRUMS, T5 MACRO) and holds only that machine's settings: Chord has play style, arp, spread, latch and the chord bass; Lead and Bass have glide; Drone has slide; Sample and Macro have their own rows; Drums has the drum page. Every machine tab starts with a Machine row, so a track can change machine right there. Settings opens on the track tab from any play mode, on Global from the Song page, on FX from the track page. Brickchord's Settings is unchanged.
- Fix: Cloud was far too heavy on the Brick (about 20 ms of a 46 ms block per track, so one cloud could push the CPU to 99% and cause dropouts). It now works on a mono buffer written twice so grains are plain slices, pitches at unison or an octave up, caps itself at six grains, and caches its windows: about 25 times cheaper.
- Macro goes analog: the Analog model now runs on the full analog engine (up to five detuned oscillators panned across the stereo field, a sub, the Moog-style ladder with its own envelope, drive), with Harmonics spreading the voices, Timbre opening the ladder and Morph moving from saw to pulse with PWM. The FM model is replaced by Sync, a hard-synced oscillator pair on the same engine. New presets: Prophet Brass, Juno Strings, Mono Lead, Supersaw, Square Bass, Saw Bass, Poly Pad, Sync Scream and Sync Bass; FM Glass, Wave Scan and Steel Drum are gone. Macro levels now match the other banks.
- Cloud (both editions): an eleventh effect in every track's rack, a granular texture processor in the spirit of the Eurorack ones. The track's last two seconds sit in a buffer and grains are read back from it, scattered, pitched and windowed. Amount sets density and mix; the rate control picks a texture instead of a time: Dust, Grit, Smear, Shimmer (octave-up grains), Haze, Drift, Bloom and Freeze (holds the last thing you played as a cloud). A track with Cloud on keeps sounding after its notes stop.
- Macro gains two exciter-and-resonator models: Bowed (a stick-slip bow) and Blown (breath), both driving a resonator bank that Harmonics bends from string to bar, with Morph narrowing its peaks. Four presets: Bowed String, Bowed Glass, Blown Pipe, Blown Bottle. Resonators now uses Bowed Glass and puts a Shimmer cloud on the Kalimba.
- Macro machine (Bricklab), a macro oscillator in the spirit of the Eurorack ones: eight models (Analog, Fold, FM, Vowel, Additive, Table, String, Modal) driven by the same three controls, Harmonics, Timbre and Morph, plus Decay for the struck models. Twelve presets: Analog Saw, Folded Sine, FM Glass, Vowel Choir, Organ Pipes, Wave Scan, Plucked String, Twin Strings, Marimba, Bell Bowl, Steel Drum, Kalimba. A Macro track opens in Play mode, so it plays chords too. Settings > Macro edits the selected track. New demo: Resonators (Marimba and Kalimba in five against four, Bell Bowl, Vowel Choir, a Plucked String bass, the Modular kit).
- Three new drum kits: Modular (west-coast low-pass-gated pings, FM blips, ring-mod metal, a laser zap), Industrial (anvils, sheet metal, an overdriven kick, crushed claps) and Glitch (clicks, stutters, chirps, crushed ticks, a hat whose sample rate falls through the note). And a demo that uses the Modular kit: Patch Bay (modular techno, A minor, 118 BPM, cross-rhythms in threes and fives, Pulse Bass with slides, FM Bell arps, a Wire lead).
- Parameter locks and slide (Bricklab): on the GRID page, hold L2 on a step: L2+UP/DOWN locks that step's filter cutoff, L2+LEFT/RIGHT sets its velocity, L2+A turns slide on, L2+B clears the step's locks. A track with any slide steps plays mono like a 303: a slide step glides the sounding note to the new pitch, and the note before it holds into the slide. Locked steps show a dot (filter) and a slash (slide). Acid Room and Analog Drift now use slides and filter locks.
- Settings has a Songs tab (Bricklab) with Song and Demo song, so Global is back to settings.
- Mixer (Bricklab): a MIX page in Song mode (L1/R1 to it) with the six tracks of the playing pattern as faders. LEFT/RIGHT picks a track, UP/DOWN sets its level, Y+LEFT/RIGHT pans, A mutes, X solos (X again restores the mutes), B copies every track's level and pan to all patterns.
- Slice mode (Bricklab): Settings > Sample > Slices (Off, 8, 16, 32) cuts the sample into equal slices; the buttons and the grid play one slice each at its own pitch. Slice to grid fills the pattern with one step per slice, so a loop recorded at any BPM plays in time with the song.
- First launch (Bricklab): a welcome screen with the five controls that matter, A to load Analog Drift into an empty song and play it, B to start empty.
- Songs name themselves the first time they have notes (key, scale and the day, like "F Minor, Nov 3"); the name shows on Settings > Global > Song.
- Eight songs (Bricklab): Settings > Global > Song picks Song 1 to 8 (LEFT/RIGHT shows what is in each, A opens it). The song you leave is saved first; an empty slot starts a new song. Each song keeps its own tempo, key and scale. Song 1 is the original brickchord_song.json, so existing songs carry over; the rest live in songs/. A demo loads into the song you are on, so pick an empty slot first to keep your work.
- Demo songs (Bricklab): Settings > Songs > Demo song: Slow Light (ambient, 64 BPM, D Dorian: sliding Tape Choir and Sub Drone, the choir sample as a slow granular cloud, vox glitter, Glass Bells and Flute in long delays, a shaker and rim for time), Analog Drift (Moog Bass, Brass and Sync leads, a Shimmer drone, a granular choir sample as the pad and pitched vox chops, F minor; its two sample WAVs ship in samples/ and are made from the synth), Night Drive (synthwave: 808, Moog Bass, Juno Pad, Fat Saw, Deep Space), Tape Lo-fi (swung Lo-fi kit, Electric Piano, Sub, Flute, D Dorian), Acid Room (909, Acid Bass, Sync Lead stabs, Dark Pad) and Cathedral (ambient: Choir Pad, Glass Bells, Cathedral drone, Brass Lead). LEFT/RIGHT picks, A loads; each sets its own tempo, key and scale, and your song is kept in brickchord_song.backup.json. tools/make_demos.py writes them; tools/soak_demos.py plays every demo headless and reports silence, clipping and engine cost, with a WAV of each.
- Fix: drones record again. With Drone slide on, the sliding voices bypassed the recorder; while a layer records, the drone now plays struck notes so every degree lands on the track (playback slides as before when you are not recording).
- Lead and Drone mode on an empty Chord track turn it into a Lead or Drone track; on a Chord track with notes they say so instead. Playing chords, leads or drones never sits on the drum track.
- Length quantize: with Rec quantize on, a held note's length snaps to the grid too, so recorded leads and bass end on a step. The profile line now has a per_track cost. Brickchord's sound list grows to 41: the 23 originals plus the Lead and Drone banks. New Bricklab artwork for the site and the KNULLI menu (tools/bricklab_art.py), and the site and README describe Bricklab as it is now.
- Sample page (Bricklab): with a Sample track selected, Settings opens on a Sample page: Start, End, Reverse, Loop, Tune, and a Grain section (Grain on/off, Grain size, Density, Position, Spray, Pitch spray). Grain renders a granular cloud into each note offline, so a one-second vocal becomes a pad or a texture that costs the same to play as a chord note. Change a setting and the next note has it.
- Drone slide (both editions): Settings > Chords > Drone slide (Off, 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 4 s; default 1 s). With it on, tapping a new degree slides every drone voice to the new pitch instead of restriking, the upper voices arriving a little after the root. Adding or removing the 3rd fades voices in and out. Off gives the old restrike.
- Machines (Bricklab). Every track is a machine: Chord, Lead, Bass, Drone or Sample (track 1 is Drums). Pick it on the track page under SOUND > Machine; the Sound row then cycles that machine's own bank, and playing the track opens the right mode (Lead for Lead, Bass and Sample, Drone for Drone). A fresh looper layer takes whatever machine you are playing.
- New sound banks with a Moog-style 4-pole ladder filter: Lead (Fat Saw, Hollow Square, Sync Lead, Sub Lead, Brass Lead, Acid, Flute, Whistle, Glass Lead, Wire), Bass (Moog Bass, Sub, Pulse Bass, Acid Bass, Reese, Pluck Bass, Hollow Bass, Fuzz Bass) and Drone (Deep Space, Tape Choir, Organ Swell, Cathedral, Dark Pad, Shimmer, Breath, Sub Drone). Chord keeps the 23 Brickchord sounds. The Sound Editor works on all of them.
- Sample machine: drop WAV files in bricklab/samples/ (kits stay in sub-folders) and a Sample track plays them pitched across the buttons from a root of C4, through the track's filter, rack and FX bus. Start, end, reverse, loop and tune are in the engine and get a settings page next.
- Bricklab has its own logo and intro: the 4 x 4 step grid drops in, the name types on, one bar of drums runs across the grid with the hit steps flashing teal, and the downbeat lands with the chord and leaves step 1 lit. The same mark sits on the Play screen and the loading screen. Any button skips the intro.
- Drone mode: the left stick does something now. Down turns the drone into tempo-synced pulses (1/8, then 1/16, then 1/32 the further you push, harder gating the deeper you go). Up is a swell with a slow stereo rotation. Left thins it to the root. Right makes the upper voices bloom and breathe. Both editions.
- Looper (Bricklab), HiChord style: press R2 (the right trigger) in any play mode to record a layer on the next empty track with the sound you are playing. The layer closes itself at the end of the pattern and loops; click again to close it early and arm the next one. Track 1 takes drum layers from Drums mode, the other five take everything else. Six lamps in the header show the layers: lit has notes, red is recording, a bar means muted. SELECT+A does the same outside Song mode; on the Song page it still records the selected track.
- Two FX buses (Bricklab). Settings > FX now has bus A and bus B, each with its own reverb type, delay type and delay time. Every track picks its bus on the track page (SENDS > FX bus), so one group of tracks can sit in a Hall with a ping-pong delay while another gets a Plate and a tape delay. A bus that nothing feeds costs nothing. Reverb types now go to the audio process by name (it renders the impulse itself), which also removes the last big message on the pipe.
- Record quantize (Bricklab): Settings > Global > Rec quantize: Off, 1/16, 1/8 or 1/4. Notes, bass and drum hits snap to that grid as they are recorded; X on the GRID page quantizes an existing track to the same grid.
- Bricklab: the play-along beat is gone. START is play/stop for the song in every mode (drums are track 1), the beat row and the logo follow the transport, Settings > Global has no Beat row, and quantize locks live chords to the transport. Brickchord keeps its START beat.
- Recording is explicit now (Bricklab). A on the Pattern page just takes you to the track to play it; nothing records unless you turn REC on with SELECT+A (and SELECT+A again turns it off). The REC chip in the header shows when a track is recording.
- A UI watchdog writes the stuck call stack to the log if the screen loop stalls for 2 seconds, and the metronome sound is now rendered on the audio side instead of being sent over the pipe (a message that large could wedge the two processes).
- One track page (Bricklab). Effects mode and SONG > X are now the same screen: the selected track of the playing pattern, with its sound, mix (level, pan, mute), sends (reverb, delay), live filter (cutoff, resonance) and the effects rack, in sections. LEFT/RIGHT sets a value, A toggles an effect (or mute, or hears the sound on the Sound row), Y+LEFT/RIGHT sets an effect's rate, L1/R1 moves between tracks, X randomizes the rack, Y+X clears it, and a tap of Y copies the whole track setup to the same track in every pattern. Before, Effects mode edited a separate live rack that was swapped under you when the pattern changed, so what you tweaked and what the track played could drift apart; Glide has moved to Settings > Chords > Lead glide.
- New drum kits. All five built-in kits (Classic, 808, 909, Lo-fi, Acoustic) were redesigned: tuned kick bodies with proper pitch drops and beater clicks, snares with shell modes and filtered wires, real 808/909-style metal for the hats, layered claps, and a small room on the Acoustic kit. They are rendered offline at 44.1 kHz by `tools/make_kits.py` and ship as WAVs in `brickchord/kits/`, so they cost the Brick nothing to play and load faster than before. Levels still follow the old Classic kit, so songs keep their mix.
- Metronome (Bricklab): Settings > Global > Metronome: Off, When recording (count-in, armed or recording) or Always (whenever the transport runs), with Click sound (Click, Wood, Beep, Rim) and Click level. It clicks quarter notes with an accent on each bar, on its own dry bus, so it is never recorded and works the same on every track. The count-in uses the same sound.
- Settings has pages now: Global, Chords, Drums (Bricklab) and FX. L1/R1 flips between them, and Settings opens on the page for what you are doing (Drums from Drums mode or the track 1 grid, FX from Effects).
- Settings > Drums, for the playing pattern's drum track: kit (this pattern or all patterns), rescan samples (new WAV kit folders show up without a relaunch), preset beat (A loads it, undoable), pattern length, swing amount and whether it swings 16ths or 8ths, accent and ghost depth, humanize (small timing and level drift), hat choke (closed hat cuts the open hat), drum level, and a mixer row per lane: LEFT/RIGHT level, Y+LEFT/RIGHT tune (+/-12 semitones), A mute, X to hear it.
- Bricklab drum machine: track 1 of every pattern is now a drum track with an 8-lane step grid (kick, snare, clap, rim, tom, hat, open hat, shaker). On SONG > GRID with track 1 selected: A toggles a step, B cycles accent / ghost, X loads the next preset beat as a starting point, Y+X changes kit, Y+UP/DOWN sets a lane's level, Y+A mutes a lane, Y+B clears it, Y+LEFT/RIGHT sets swing. Every pattern keeps its own drums, so songs get real verses and fills. The Drums pads play and record into track 1, through its effects.
- Drum kits: Classic, 808, 909, Lo-fi and Acoustic, plus your own WAV kits in `samples/<kit name>/` (kick.wav, snare.wav, hat.wav ...; levels matched automatically). See samples_README.txt.
- The START beat steps aside while a pattern's drum track is playing, so the two never double up.
- Older songs: notes recorded on track 1 move to the first empty track.
- Brickchord: Drums mode removed (it didn't connect to the beat). The preset beat stays on START.
- Mode wheel: click the left stick (L3) to open it, push the stick toward Play, Lead, Drone, Drums, Sequencer or Effects, let go to switch. L3 is learned once on first launch.
- New synth engines: FM (FM Keys, FM Bell, FM Brass), Karplus-Strong plucked strings (Nylon Guitar, Steel Guitar), Drawbar Organ, Vox Pad (formant vowels that morph) and Air Pad (shaped noise). 23 sounds in total.
- Effects rack with LFOs, stackable: Drive, Lo-fi, Chorus, Flanger, Vibrato, Tremolo, Gate, Auto-Pan, Wide (stereo) and Filter LFO, each with an amount and a tempo-synced rate (1/16 to 4 bars), plus Glide. Randomize with X. Runs alongside the existing reverbs and delays.
- Lead mode: single-note melodies on the 7 chord buttons, Y/L1 octave up/down, R1 sharp, left stick bends and adds vibrato, legato glide.
- Drone mode: tap a degree to sustain a drone of that chord root (Y adds the 3rd), play other modes on top.
- Drums mode: 8 finger-drum pads (kick, snare, hats, clap, tom, rim, shaker), L1 soft, R1 accent.
- Repeat play modes: Repeat 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 retrigger the held chord in time.
- Two apps from one codebase: Brickchord (the chord machine, no song mode) and Bricklab (Brickchord plus song mode). A file `brickchord/edition.txt` containing `lab` makes a folder run as Bricklab; Bricklab.sh launches it from `ports/bricklab/`, sharing Brickchord's sound cache.
- Bricklab: count-in for recording (Settings > Count-in: Off, 1 or 2 bars). With a count-in, clicks play and recording starts on the one; if the transport is already running it starts at the next pattern.
- Reverb runs at half the sample rate (about twice as fast; it was the biggest cost on the Brick).
- Song mode (replaces the looper): 16 patterns of 1 to 8 bars, each with 6 tracks that record what you play. Every track in every pattern keeps its own sound, effects rack, filter, level, pan and sends. Pages: PATTERN (mixer, Y+LEFT/RIGHT length, Y+A copy to the next empty pattern), GRID (step edit: add, delete, transpose, length, quantize, all undoable), SONG (chain patterns with repeats). Patterns switch at the end of the bar, Digitone style.
- Live playing goes through the selected track, so the Effects page, Settings > Sound / Reverb / Delay and the right stick act on that track and what you hear is what it records. (Before, recorded tracks ignored the Effects page.)
- Hold SELECT in any mode: A record, B undo/redo, X play/stop, Y mute, UP/DOWN track, LEFT/RIGHT pattern. A tap on SELECT still opens Settings.
- The audio engine now runs in its own process (audio_proc.py) so drawing can't make the sound drop out. The log's status lines include per-stage engine timings.
- Songs are saved to brickchord_song.json.
- Fix: harp sweeps, chord spread and strums could raise an engine error (a sequencer variable overwrote the event counter).
- Not yet tested on hardware.

## v0.1.0-alpha (2026-10-31)
First public alpha.
- 15 sounds: adds Wide Supersaw, Prophet Poly, Electric Piano, Glass Bells, Choir Pad, Acid Chord, Lofi Strings, Sub Pad
- Sounds load almost instantly from a disk cache. `factory_render.py` pre-renders all 15 stock sounds on a computer (copy `brickchord/cache` to the handheld); edited sounds render in the background only while nothing is playing, so play never crackles
- Fix: sound dropping out mid-play (notes are no longer rendered during play, voice count is capped, the audio stream restarts itself if it ever runs dry)
- Sound Editor (Settings > Edit Sound): wave, detune, sub, filter, envelopes, PWM; edits saved per sound
- Diatonic chord buttons, button and stick modifiers, 4 scales
- Analog-VA engine, filter and resonance controls
- Sample-accurate audio engine: shared clock for drums, strums, arps and harp sweeps
- 8 reverb types (Hall, Room, Plate, Big Hall, Spring, Reverse, Gated, Lo-fi Dark) and 6 delay types (Ping-Pong, Straight, Tape, Dub, Slapback, Multi-Tap), plus triplet delay times
- Effects only touch the synth; drums stay dry
- 27 beats incl. Jungle, Techno, 2-Step Garage, UK Funky, Neo-Soul, Afro House, Footwork; new clap, rim, tom and shaker sounds; per-beat swing
- Right stick: up = more reverb and delay together, down = dry, left = live filter sweep, right = resonance sweep; click the stick to hold the effects
- Play modes: Chord, Strum Folk/Pop/Skank, Slop, Harp Up/Down, 5 arpeggiators
- Quantize, latch, spread, latency settings; per-launch log files

## Logo
- New button-grid logo (eight keys, one orange), shown on the loading screen and in the EmulationStation ports list. Source SVGs in docs/.
- The logo animates at launch: keys pop in like a strum, the chord is struck, then a step light walks the keys while sounds load. Any button skips it.
- Startup sound is now a strummed chord in your key (home chord with its 7th, bell/e-piano tone through reverb and delay), landing when the logo keys are struck. Replaces the two-tone beep.
- Everything animates: chord names drop in letter by letter, struck tiles shatter into bricks, a self-playing Tetris well behind the chord name hard-drops a piece on every chord (shape = chord degree), clears lines in a burst of bricks and falls in time with the beat. Live oscilloscope, gliding FX bars, keys that fade out, menus whose rows drop in.
- Faster drawing: cached text, bricks and keyboard, a much cheaper Tetris AI, 15 fps at rest, and the UI backs off when the audio gets busy. Fixes the lag and crackles the animations caused.
- Bass: a dedicated synth bass on the dry bus (plucky saw + solid sine fundamental), with Bass modes Synth, Pulse, Octaves, Walk, Sub + Patch, Patch, Off and a Bass Level. Arp Octaves setting (1 to 4, default 2).

## R36Max port
- Square 720x720 layout (bigger brick well, taller keys and keyboard, more menu rows), picked automatically from the screen size.
- `vendor.py` finds NumPy and pygame: bundled wheels for aarch64 Python 3.7-3.12 are unpacked on first launch; system pygame is preferred.
- `r36/` holds the ArkOS-family launcher, install notes and `build_zip.sh`.

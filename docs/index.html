Chord machine for TrimUI Brick Pro · KNULLI

Seven keys. Every chord in your key.

Brickchord turns a handheld into a pocket chord instrument. Each button plays one chord that fits your song, the sticks bend and color it, and a drum machine and synth bass keep everything on the beat.

Download v0.3.0 Source on GitHub

Free and open source (MIT). Runs as a Port on KNULLI. Alpha: expect rough edges. What's new in 0.3.0

On the device

A screen that plays along

Everything on Brickchord's screen moves with the music. This capture is the real app drawing itself.

  • A Tetris well behind every chord. Each chord slams a piece down, its shape set by the chord you played. Full lines burst into bricks, and pieces fall one row per beat.
  • Keys that shatter. The chord tile you hit sinks, flashes and throws bricks, then fades back slowly instead of snapping off.
  • Letters that drop in. The chord name falls in one letter at a time and lands with a bounce.
  • A live oscilloscope. The output waveform runs faintly behind the chord name, and the effect bars glide as you move the stick.
  • A launch chord. The logo animates on start and plays the home chord of your key as its keys are struck.

What's inside

An analog-style synth, a bass and a drum machine

Every note is rendered from detuned saws, pulse waves and a sub oscillator through a resonant filter. Effects touch only the synth, so the drums and bass stay punchy and dry.

The bigger one

Want a looper, patterns and machines? That's Bricklab.

Same engine, same buttons, built out into a pocket groovebox: a looper you layer from any mode, 16 patterns of 6 tracks, Moog-style leads and basses, samples with a granular mode, a drum machine, and effects on every track.

New in the beta

Play it from a keyboard

All three apps now take USB MIDI in. Plug a class-compliant keyboard or controller into the Brick's USB-C: one key plays the chord on that degree, like its button; several keys together play that exact chord. Tested with the Telepathic Instruments Orchid.

The ambient one

Want chords that glide? That's Brickglide.

The same seven buttons, but press a chord and five voices slide into it and hold it. Let the progression play and the buttons become a melody that always fits, the stick strums and arpeggiates, and your MIDI keyboard and samples join in. Now in beta.

Install on KNULLI

Four steps to your first chord

  1. Download brickchord-v0.3.0-knulli.zip from GitHub Releases. It bundles the ARM64 NumPy Brickchord needs.
  2. Copy brickchord/ and Brickchord.sh into roms/ports/ on the SD card (/userdata/roms/ports/ on the device).
  3. On the device, open Start → Game Settings → Update Gamelists, then launch Ports → Brickchord.
  4. On first launch, press each button once and push each stick once so Brickchord learns your controls.

Want every stock sound to open instantly? On a computer with Python 3 and NumPy, run python3 brickchord/factory_render.py in the release folder before copying it over. It pre-renders all 15 sounds (about 355 MB). Otherwise the Brick renders each sound in quiet moments the first time you use it and keeps it for next time. The pack matches sounds with Filter and Resonance at 0 in Settings; use the right stick for live filter moves.

Tested firmware: knulli-a133-trimui-brick-pro-beta-20260921. KNULLI for the Brick Pro is still in beta. Get it from the KNULLI project (public builds are on their GitHub releases); Brickchord doesn't host or support the firmware itself.

Updating from 0.1? Copy the new files over the old ones. Your settings and sound edits are kept.
